Scope of Expertise

Five technical core trades, one integrated team.

HVAC — Climate Systems

Split, multi-split, VRV/VRF systems, air handling units (AHU), single and dual-flow mechanical ventilation (VMC), gas/oil-fired boilers, and air-water or water-water heat pumps. Category I F-Gas certification.

Electrical Infrastructure

Main low-voltage switchboards (TGBT), sub-panels, emergency escape lighting (BAES), surge protection, annual thermographic inspections, and statutory safety audit compliance (Q18/Q19 clearances).

Plumbing & Commercial Sanitation

Domestic hot and cold water distribution pipework, system flushing, legionella prevention and treatment (sanitary logbook updates, regular sampling), accessible facilities, and automated shut-off valves.

Low Voltage & Technical Security

Fire detection and alarm systems, certified intrusion detection systems, technical alert alarms (server room temperature fluctuations, water leaks), intercoms, and public address audio setups.

BMS / Automation (GTB)

Complete building management system supervision under open architectures. Multi-protocol integration (BACnet, Modbus TCP/RTU, KNX, M-Bus). Energy consumption analytics, context-aware alarms, and remote steering.

Regulatory Testing Coordination

Liaising with third-party inspection bureaus (Apave, Bureau Veritas, Socotec, etc.), processing compliance logs, managing certifications, and correcting noted defects. We track the calendar so you can sign off with confidence.

Our Underlying Principle: A modern facility is a complex, unified **ecosystem**, not an independent collection of separate parts. A minor water leak can short out a main switchboard; an AHU breakdown can compromise a server stack. **We evaluate the entire picture** and execute where it matters most.

Frequently Asked Questions — Multi-technical

Critical parameters verified by technical directors.

What does "multi-technical" cover?
It encapsulates all physical engineering assets within a property: HVAC (heating, ventilation, cooling plants), electrical infrastructure (main distribution switchboards, sub-panels, emergency lighting systems), plumbing (sanitation, sludge purging, anti-legionella testing loops), low voltage assets (fire protection systems, security alarms, smart access), and BMS architectures.
Do you have an in-house field team or do you outsource?
Our engineers are fully salaried, in-house technical personnel possessing complete working credentials (electrical safety certifications, F-Gas credentials, and working at heights training). We only leverage highly vetted specialized subcontractors for extremely niche trades (such as vertical elevator certifiers, industrial rope-access teams, or licensed asbestos removal), managed with 100% transparency.
Can you take over the maintenance of an existing facility?
Yes, this represents a significant portion of our portfolio. We launch every project with a detailed technical baseline audit to capture missing compliance checks, structural weaknesses, and wear issues, enabling a smooth, risk-free handover with a prioritize remediation roadmap if required.
Do you support open-architecture BMS platforms?
Yes. We engineer and map assets exclusively via open protocols including BACnet, Modbus TCP/RTU, KNX, and M-Bus. You avoid vendor lock-in or proprietary software traps, allowing you to retain full agility over your building assets.
Are your technicians certified to handle refrigerants?
Yes, our engineering roster holds Category I F-Gas certifications, giving them full authority to commission, service, and de-gas any size of commercial cooling equipment. Complete logbook compliance and statutory fluid tracking reports are inherently included within your basic service scope.
How do you handle the transition from my current contractor?
We deploy an onboarding phase lasting 1 to 3 months depending on site complexity. During this period, we extract physical facility drawings, verify subcontractor histories, and import past logs into our CMMS system. This ensures absolute continuity of service with zero downtime.
